Chapter 4

The ship is in. Now the clocks start.

Once your container is unloaded, three deadlines run at the same time. Miss them and you pay storage every day, or worse.

A few daysThe shipping line's free time. One carrier's notice says that after 3 days it can move your container to bonded storage, at your cost.
15 daysTo file your customs declaration. You can ask for 15 more, but only before the first 15 run out.
30 daysUncollected goods can be treated as abandoned and sold. Don't let it get anywhere near this.

Your first week, in order

  1. Email the shipping line's import desk. Ask for the actual unloading date, their invoice of local charges, your free days, and where to return the empty container. Contacts are on the Carriers page.
  2. Send their customer information sheet. It's written for companies. Fill it in as an individual: name, address, mobile, TIN. Mark PEZA "No".
  3. Pay their charges and the container deposit. Hand over the original B/L, or sign the Letter of Undertaking for a telex release.
  4. Get the delivery order. The terminal won't release your container without it.
  5. Go to customs at the port where the container is (Chapter 5). For Manila, check whether it's MICT/MICP in Tondo or South Harbor.

Arrives on a weekend?

The port runs every day, but customs and shipping-line offices may be closed. Spend the weekend on paperwork, and be at the window first thing Monday.

Need more time to file?

Ask customs for the 15-day extension in writing, before day 15. Our AI prompt drafts the request.
