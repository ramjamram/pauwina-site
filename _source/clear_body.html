How to Clear a Container of Household Goods in Manila

You have an arrival notice on your phone, you've already paid your mover abroad, and now strangers are asking for documents. Here's who to contact, what to send, and what you may still owe.

Start here: do these today

  1. On your arrival notice, find the bill of lading (B/L) number, container number, port of discharge, and the carrier's import contact.
  2. Email the shipping line and ask them to confirm in writing: B/L release status, their charges, the container deposit, your last free day, and where to return the empty. Copy our message
  3. Open a ticket with Customs (BOC) at your port, asking for an assessment and whether you qualify for returning-resident privileges. Copy our message
  4. Start your valued inventory with photos. See the example
  5. Reread your signed mover's quote: where does their service end, and what's excluded?
  6. Put three dates in your calendar: the discharge date, your last free day, and 15 days after discharge (the customs filing deadline).

Work on the shipping line and Customs at the same time. Neither one waits for the other.

1. "I paid my mover. Why is a shipping line contacting me?"

Because your mover's job usually ends before your container reaches your door. Here's a typical handoff, anonymized from a real shipment:

Case example

A family in [US STATE] paid a moving company to pack their home. The mover trucked the goods to a freight forwarder near [US PORT]. The forwarder booked the container with an ocean carrier and issued the paperwork. When the ship reached Manila, it was the carrier, not the mover, that emailed the arrival notice and asked for documents and payment.

Check your signed quote Find the destination it covers (door, port or warehouse) and the exclusions. "Destination charges," "duties and taxes," "storage" and "delivery" are often excluded. A warehouse-to-warehouse or port-to-port quote can leave the final trip home for you to arrange separately.

2. "I have my arrival notice. What do I do first?"

Copy these from your arrival notice

Three dates that sound alike

Estimated arrivalThe date the ship is expected. It often moves.
Actual arrivalThe date the ship really reaches the port. Your container may still be on board.
DischargeThe date your container is unloaded. Free days and the 15-day customs filing deadline usually count from here. Ask for it in writing.

Two tracks, at the same time

Track A

Carrier release

  1. Email the carrier (message below).
  2. Get their invoice and pay it exactly as instructed.
  3. Settle the container deposit.
  4. Send what they ask for to release the B/L.
  5. Receive the delivery order.
Track B

Customs

  1. Register on your port's BOC portal.
  2. Open a ticket asking for an assessment (message below).
  3. Send your inventory, photos and IDs.
  4. Follow the instructions you get: inspection, payment, release.
  5. Keep every official receipt.

Message to the shipping line

Send to the import email on your arrival notice. Replace everything in [BRACKETS].

Subject: Import release – B/L [B/L NUMBER], container [CONTAINER NUMBER]

Good day,

I am the named consignee for this shipment of used household goods:

B/L: [B/L NUMBER]
Container: [CONTAINER NUMBER]
Vessel/voyage: [VESSEL / VOYAGE]
Port of discharge: [PORT]

Please confirm in writing:
1. The release status of the B/L, and anything still needed to release it.
2. Your local charges, with an invoice I can pay.
3. The container deposit amount and how to settle it.
4. The actual discharge date and my last free day before storage, demurrage or detention.
5. Where and how to return the empty container, and what receipt proves the return.
6. What I need to submit to get the delivery order.

Thank you,
[YOUR NAME]

3. "What does the shipping line need?"

Each carrier has its own process. Here's what one carrier publishes, and what actually happened to us.

What MSC Philippines lists for online delivery-order processing
  • Container guarantee
  • Authorization letter (MSC's page says "for broker")
  • B/L copy
  • ID of the authorized signatories and representatives
  • Payment deposit slips, following their bills-payment instructions
Source: MSC Philippines local information, checked November 2026. MSC also says payments go through a Metrobank bills-payment facility, for the exact invoice amount, with the invoice reference as the account number.
Case example · MSC Philippines · November 2026
  • Two separate steps. Getting our account approved on MSC's online system (QWIIK) was one step. Requesting the delivery order was another.
  • An approved account showed an empty "Imports" list. Approval didn't mean our shipment was linked to the account yet.
  • MSC asked us for a B/L copy, an authorization letter, a container guaranty, the signatory's ID, and proof of payment.
  • A payment slip we uploaded during account verification still had to be sent again as part of the shipment release.
  • MSC directed us to the Container Ledger Account (CLA) for the container deposit, a separate system from MSC's own.

Three different kinds of money

WhatPaid toDo you get it back?
Carrier's local chargesThe shipping lineNo. It's a fee for their services in the Philippines.
Container depositThe shipping line, or through CLAYes, after the empty container is returned, minus any detention or damage charges.
Duties and taxesThe Bureau of CustomsNo. But you may qualify for an exemption; see question 4.

About CLA: it's an online system for container deposits that shipping lines join voluntarily. Its FAQ says it covers container detention and damage charges, and aims to refund deposits within 3 working days after the shipping line approves. CLA FAQ. We don't list amounts here because they change. Ask the carrier and CLA for the current figure in writing.

If you have no broker: ask before you sign

Carriers' forms often assume a broker. Send this before you upload anything.

Good day,

Re: B/L [B/L NUMBER], container [CONTAINER NUMBER]

I am the named consignee and am handling this shipment personally. What should I upload for the authorization letter, and who should sign the container guaranty?

Thank you,
[YOUR NAME]

Signing a carrier's form as the consignee doesn't make you, or the signer, a customs broker. It's the owner of the goods dealing with the carrier. Whether MSC accepts a consignee's own letter, or another form, in place of a broker's authorization was still unconfirmed in our case. Ask them, and get the answer in writing.

4. "How do I ask Customs for an assessment?"

Customs works through online support tickets. You need a registered account before you can open one, and each ticket gets a number you can track.

PortBOC portal
Manila International Container Port (MICP)micpportal.customs.gov.ph
Port of Manila (South Harbor)pomportal.customs.gov.ph
NAIA, Batangas, Davaocares.customs.gov.ph
Other portsclient.customs.gov.ph (BOC Client Portal)

Check which port your container is under on your arrival notice. The general BOC-CARES helpline is (02) 8705-6000.

Case example · MICP · November 2026
  • BOC-CARES told us to send our inquiry to the District Collector of the port concerned, which for our container was MICP.
  • The path we used: BOC Client Portal → Open a New Ticket → Office of the DISTRICT COLLECTOR.
  • We forgot one file. Replying on the same ticket with the passport PDF attached worked: it appeared in the ticket thread.
  • Save your ticket number somewhere you'll find it.
  • After uploading, check that each filename shows in the ticket.
  • An open ticket only confirms Customs received your inquiry. Clearance, an exemption decision and an assessment are separate outcomes, and each comes later.

Ticket to the District Collector

Attach your B/L, arrival notice, inventory with photos, and the documents in the checklist below.

Subject: Request for assessment – used household goods – B/L [B/L NUMBER], container [CONTAINER NUMBER]

Good day,

I am the consignee of a 20-foot container of used household goods:

Consignee: [YOUR FULL NAME, AS ON PASSPORT]
B/L: [B/L NUMBER]
Container: [CONTAINER NUMBER]
Vessel/voyage: [VESSEL / VOYAGE]
Port: [PORT, e.g. MICP]
Discharge date: [DATE]
My arrival in the Philippines: [DATE]

I respectfully ask:
1. Am I eligible for the returning-resident privilege on personal and household effects, given my arrival date and the shipment's arrival date?
2. May I have an itemized assessment of any duties and taxes due?
3. May I file and process this shipment personally as the consignee?
4. If I need to appear in person, where, and what should I bring?

Attached: [LIST YOUR ATTACHMENTS].

Thank you,
[YOUR NAME]
[YOUR MOBILE NUMBER]

About returning-resident privileges: the official rules say the goods should arrive within 60 days of your return. If yours came months after you did, ask the District Collector, and the Department of Finance Revenue Office that issues the exemption, how they'll treat it. We can't promise eligibility. 5. "How do I document what my used furniture is worth?"

Customs charges duties on what it decides your goods are worth today. A clear, honest inventory with photos is your best evidence.

  • Use original photos. If you brightened one to make it readable, label it "edited for visibility" and keep the original.
  • Secondhand or free items count too. Say how you got them.
  • If you used a depreciation method to estimate values, say it's your own estimate. There's no fixed yearly depreciation rate we know of that Customs publishes for used household goods.
  • If your detailed list differs from an earlier estimate (on a mover's form, say), show both figures and explain why they differ.

6. "Can I empty the container at the pier and hire a truck for the furniture?"

Unloading a container is called stripping. Doing it inside the port lets you skip hiring a container hauler for the whole box, but it needs permission, and you'll be charged for handling.

Container hauler

A tractor-trailer that moves the loaded container out of the port to your home, then returns the empty.

Truck for loose goods

A regular truck that carries your furniture and boxes after the container has been emptied at the port.
Case example · November 2026 The customs portal showed an option called "Office of the DepColl Operations / Request for Stripping." Seeing that option didn't mean we had permission to unload at the terminal. We requested unloading at the terminal, and approval was still pending when we wrote this.
Questions to ask before you book a truck
  1. Who approves stripping, and at what location?
  2. Who is allowed to do the unloading: terminal workers, or my own helpers?
  3. Can my chosen truck enter the terminal, and what pass does it need?
  4. What handling or stripping charges apply, and who bills them?
  5. Who records the empty container's return, and what receipt proves it?
Ask Customs (on your ticket), the terminal operator, and the shipping line, since each controls part of this.

Still open in our case

  • Whether MSC accepts a consignee's own authorization letter. Ask: MSC import desk.
  • Whether stripping at the terminal is approved. Ask: the District Collector, on your ticket, and the terminal.
  • Eligibility for returning-resident privileges on a late shipment. Ask: the District Collector and the DOF Revenue Office.
