Glossary & links

What do all these words mean?

Shipping and customs come with their own language. Here it is in plain English, plus the official sources we use.

ABCDEFGHILMNOPRSTVW
Abandonment
When goods aren't claimed within the legal period after unloading, customs can treat them as abandoned and sell them. Avoid it by filing within 15 days.
Arrastre
The port's charge for handling your container at the terminal: lifting it off the ship, moving it in the yard. Paid to the terminal operator.
Arrival notice
The email or PDF the shipping line sends shortly before your ship docks, with the ETA, terminal, bill of lading number and release conditions.
Assessment
Customs' calculation of the value of your goods and the duties and taxes you owe. Always ask for it in writing.
Balikbayan
A Filipino returning home from abroad. Also used for the famous balikbayan boxes of gifts and personal goods.
Balikbayan box
A carton of personal effects sent home by a Filipino abroad. Covered by its own duty-free rules, separate from a full household container.
Bill of lading (B/L)
The shipping document that works as the receipt, the contract and the ownership title for your container. Whoever holds it can claim the goods.
BOC
Bureau of Customs, the Philippine agency that clears imports and collects duties.
Bonded storage
A customs-controlled warehouse where uncleared goods can be moved, at the importer's cost, if not collected in time.
Brokerage fee
The customs broker's own professional fee, separate from duties, VAT, port charges and trucking. The official schedule (CAO 1-2001) sets ₱469.22 to ₱700 per informal entry.
CAO / CMO
Customs Administrative Order / Customs Memorandum Order: the rules that put the customs law into practice. CAO 06-2016 and CMO 15-2017 cover returning residents.
CMTA
Customs Modernization and Tariff Act (Republic Act 10863), the main Philippine customs law. Section 800(f) covers returning residents' household goods.
Consignee
The person named on the bill of lading as receiving the goods. Only the consignee (or their authorized agent) can clear the container.
Container deposit
Money the shipping line holds until you return the empty container. Refundable, minus any late-return charges.
Customs broker
A PRC-licensed professional (RA 9280) who prepares and files customs declarations for others. Optional for household goods if you're the consignee.
Delivery order (DO)
The shipping line's release paper. The terminal won't hand over your container without it.
Demurrage
Daily charges when a full container stays at the port past its free days.
Detention
Daily charges when you keep the shipping line's container outside the port too long before returning it empty.
DOF Revenue Office
The Department of Finance office that issues the Tax Exemption Indorsement for returning residents' duty-free goods.
Dutiable value
The value customs uses to calculate duties. It can include freight and insurance, not just the goods themselves.
Duties
Import tax on goods. For used household goods, often around 15%, plus 12% VAT.
Express release / telex release
An electronic release of the bill of lading, so no paper original has to be couriered. Usually needs a signed Letter of Undertaking.
FCL / LCL
Full Container Load (the whole container is yours) / Less than Container Load (you share a container with others).
Formal entry
The full customs declaration process for commercial goods. Household goods usually go through informal entry instead.
Forwarder
The company you hire to pick up your goods and book them on a ship. It may issue its own 'house' bill of lading.
Free time / free days
The days the shipping line or terminal gives you before storage, demurrage or detention charges start.
Gate pass
The terminal's permission for your container to leave the port.
House B/L
A bill of lading issued by a forwarder, as opposed to the master B/L issued by the shipping line.
Informal entry
The simpler customs process that covers personal and household effects not in commercial quantity.
Letter of Undertaking (LOU)
A letter you sign for the shipping line accepting its terms, usually needed for a telex or express release.
MICP / MICT
Manila International Container Port (the customs district) / Manila International Container Terminal (the terminal in Tondo).
Notify party
The person the shipping line contacts when the cargo arrives. Often the same as the consignee.
OFW
Overseas Filipino Worker. Returning OFWs get extra duty-free privileges for home appliances.
PHED
Personal and Household Effects Declaration, the customs form listing what's in your shipment, used for the returning-resident exemption.
Port of discharge
The port where your container is unloaded, such as Manila North Harbour. It can differ from the terminal where you pick it up.
Returning resident
A Filipino national returning after at least 6 months abroad, including a dependent spouse and children. Eligible for duty-free household goods up to a limit.
Seal number
The number on the security seal locking your container. Photograph it before it's cut.
Tax Exemption Indorsement (TEI)
The DOF document that supports your claim to the duty-free privilege. Formerly called a Tax Exemption Certificate.
TEU
Twenty-foot Equivalent Unit: one 20-foot container. A 40-footer is 2 TEU.
TIN
Taxpayer Identification Number from the BIR, needed for customs filings.
VAT
Value-Added Tax: 12% in the Philippines, charged on imports on top of duties.
Wharfage
A Philippine Ports Authority fee for use of the port, charged per container.
