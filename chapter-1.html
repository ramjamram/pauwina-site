Chapter 1

Before the truck comes

Most of the trouble at Manila customs starts right here, in your driveway. A few hours of paperwork now can save you weeks, and a lot of pesos, later.

Decide whose name goes on it

The person named as consignee on the bill of lading is the one who can clear the container. If someone in your family is a returning Filipino, put their name, exactly as it appears in their passport. Only they can claim the duty-free allowance.

Make the list customs actually wants

Movers' inventory forms are usually handwritten, and they never have prices. Customs wants every item, a plain description, and what it's worth today, used.

What to leave out

  • Anything in commercial quantity: ten of the same thing looks like stock for selling.
  • Medicines beyond small personal amounts. Hand-carry those.
  • Plants, seeds and soil, which need a Bureau of Plant Industry permit.
  • Pets, which need a Bureau of Animal Industry permit and travel separately.
  • A vehicle, unless it has its own bill of lading and import permit.

Questions to ask your forwarder

  1. When does it actually sail, not just get picked up? From which port?
  2. Can I have an express or telex release instead of a paper original? Then nothing has to be couriered.
  3. Who is your agent in Manila, and who are their contacts?
  4. What's included, and what will I pay on arrival?
  5. Are you on DTI's list of accredited sea-freight forwarders? See Carriers.

Before you go, keep copies of

Your passport photo page · your US address proof · purchase receipts for big items · the signed mover's inventory · your forwarder's invoice and booking
