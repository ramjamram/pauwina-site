Chapter 3

The 60-day rule nobody warns you about

You've packed the container and booked your flight. Here's the part that decides whether you pay nothing at customs, or tens of thousands of pesos.

What the rule says

If you're a Filipino coming home after living abroad, the law lets you bring your used household goods in duty- and tax-free, up to a limit. There's a catch: the goods have to arrive within 60 days of the day you land.

The clock starts with your arrival stamp, not the day the container ships.

Time you lived abroadDuty-free up to
6 months to under 5 years₱150,000
5 to under 10 years₱250,000
10 years or more₱350,000

A dependent spouse and children count too. Anything over the limit is taxed. You apply through the DOF Revenue Office for a Tax Exemption Indorsement, and declare your goods on the PHED form.

How families miss it

It's rarely carelessness. Your goods get picked up, sit in a warehouse, get trucked to a port in another state, then wait for a ship. From the US East Coast, the ocean trip alone can take two months.

How to stay inside the window

Ship your container before you fly. Book pickup 6–8 weeks ahead of your flight, and ask the forwarder for the real sailing date, not just the pickup date. If the timing can't work, ask whether a West Coast port is faster.

What if I already missed it?

We'll be straight with you: the customs rules don't describe an extension or an appeal for late shipments. But they don't forbid asking either. Here's what to try, in order:

  1. Ask in writing. Write to the DOF Revenue Office and the Bureau of Customs at your port, requesting the exemption anyway. Explain that the delay came from the shipping chain, not from you. Draft it with AI
  2. Attach proof. Booking confirmation, pickup receipt, warehouse and trucking records, the bill of lading dates, and your passport stamps.
  3. If they say no, make the value fair. Duties are charged on what customs says your goods are worth. A clear, itemized list of used values matters more than anything else.
  4. Check the assessment before you pay. If the values look wrong, ask for the computation in writing. You can file a formal protest, but the deadline is short, so ask customs for it on the spot.

Where this comes from: CMTA (RA 10863) Section 800(f) · CAO 06-2016 · CMO 15-2017 (Annex A). Official links
